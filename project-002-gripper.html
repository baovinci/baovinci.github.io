Mechanical Design

Adaptive Robotic Gripper

Design and structural analysis of an underactuated compliant gripper for delicate object manipulation, validated through FEA and 3D-printed prototypes.

FEA SolidWorks MATLAB Robotics

01 — Overview

Design Objective

Traditional rigid grippers require precise force control to avoid damaging fragile objects. This project developed an underactuated compliant mechanism that passively adapts to object geometry, distributing grip force evenly across contact surfaces without active force feedback.

02 — Design

Mechanism Architecture

The gripper employs a four-bar linkage combined with flexural hinges to achieve underactuation. Three phalanges per finger provide progressive enveloping, with contact force determined by tendon routing geometry rather than active motor control.

03 — FEA Verification

Structural Analysis

Finite element analysis was performed in SolidWorks Simulation to evaluate stress distribution in flexural hinges under maximum loading. Peak von Mises stress remained below 60% of the material yield strength (ABS), confirming design robustness for repeated cycling.

04 — Prototype

Physical Validation

A functional prototype was 3D-printed in ABS and tested with objects ranging from 15 mm to 45 mm in diameter. The gripper successfully handled eggs, glass vials, and deformable objects without damage across 100+ test cycles.

05 — Insight

Key Takeaway

"Compliant mechanisms offer a compelling alternative to sensor-heavy robotic end-effectors — passive mechanical intelligence, when properly designed through FEA-guided iteration, can achieve robust performance with simpler control architectures."
