Structural Component Optimization

Density-based topology optimization of a structural bracket using the SIMP method, achieving significant mass reduction while maintaining structural integrity under multi-axial loading conditions.

Topology Opt FEA Optimization MATLAB Structural

01 — Overview

Optimization Objective

Structural brackets in mechanical systems must withstand severe multi-axial loads while contributing minimally to overall system mass. This project applied density-based topology optimization (SIMP method) to redesign a structural bracket for weight-critical applications.

02 — Methodology

SIMP Framework

The Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization (SIMP) method was implemented in MATLAB. The objective function minimized compliance subject to a 40% volume fraction constraint, with sensitivity filtering (rmin = 1.5 × element size) to prevent checkerboard patterns.

03 — Load Cases

Multi-Axial Loading

Four critical load cases were considered: vertical, lateral, torsional, and combined multi-axial loading. Each load case was weighted in the objective function based on occurrence probability.

04 — Optimization Results

Convergence & Topology

The optimization converged in 78 iterations, producing an organic truss-like topology that differed significantly from the baseline design. The optimized geometry was reconstructed as a CAD model for FEA validation.

05 — Verification

FEA Verification

The optimized geometry was re-analyzed using FEA with a fine tetrahedral mesh. Von Mises stress distributions confirmed that all regions operated below the material fatigue limit with a minimum safety factor of 2.1 across all load cases.

06 — Insight

Key Takeaway

"Topology optimization is not a push-button solution — constraint formulation and load case weighting are as critical as the numerical solver. A poorly weighted multi-load optimization can converge to a design that is optimal on paper but impractical to manufacture."

— From the project's design-for-manufacturing assessment
