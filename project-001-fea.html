Nonlinear FEA of Elastic-Plastic Behavior

Finite element analysis of elastic-plastic deformation in a structural component under cyclic loading, with material hardening, large deformation, and contact nonlinearity using Abaqus.

FEA Abaqus MATLAB Nonlinear Structural

01 — Overview

Project Background

Structural components in mechanical systems frequently experience loading conditions that drive material beyond the elastic limit, resulting in permanent plastic deformation. This project investigates the nonlinear elastic-plastic behavior of a structural component under cyclic loading using finite element analysis.

The analysis incorporates three sources of nonlinearity: material nonlinearity through isotropic hardening plasticity, geometric nonlinearity due to large deformation, and contact nonlinearity at mating interfaces. The objective was to characterize plastic strain accumulation and stress redistribution across multiple loading cycles.

02 — Methodology

Numerical Framework

An implicit static analysis was performed in Abaqus/Standard using the Newton-Raphson incremental-iterative solution procedure. The elastic-plastic material model employed the von Mises yield criterion with isotropic hardening, calibrated to experimental stress-strain data.

Contact pairs were defined between mating component surfaces using the penalty method with a finite-sliding formulation. Automatic time incrementation with line search was enabled to handle convergence difficulties during plastic flow and contact status changes.

03 — Mesh & Setup

Computational Domain

The component was discretized using C3D8R reduced-integration hexahedral elements to balance accuracy and computational cost. A mesh convergence study was conducted for the nonlinear problem, with refinement applied in regions of anticipated high stress gradients and contact interfaces.

04 — Results

Deformation & Stress Analysis

Equivalent plastic strain (PEEQ) distributions revealed localized plastic deformation in regions of geometric discontinuity. Stress concentration zones were identified and correlated with the onset of plastic flow. The load-displacement curves exhibited a clear elastic-plastic transition with progressive hardening across successive loading cycles.

05 — Key Insight

Engineering Takeaway

"Nonlinear FEA demands careful attention to convergence behavior — automatic time stepping with line search proved essential for navigating elastic-plastic transitions, while mesh refinement in contact regions was the dominant factor controlling solution accuracy in this class of problems."
